Method

  Called the candidate's actual Aqara.login() method against the US service.
  The first three calls used the same fabricated account and password. The
  original and random-key controls changed only the client configuration;
  the candidate's request-signing and login implementation ran in every case.
  The final call used a dedicated maintainer-controlled account configured for
  the USA region, confirmed in Aqara Home and at the local test prompt.

  There was one request per test, four requests total, without retries.
  Credentials were entered locally, and neither they nor session values were
  written to the result files. Only fixed classifications, result codes,
  timestamps, booleans, endpoint, and source hashes were retained.

Conclusion and limits

  The original USA signing configuration reproduces the reported code 106.
  The candidate reaches account validation and successfully signs in with a
  USA-region account. The random-key control still fails signature validation.
  This independently verifies the specific login defect tracked in issue #40.

  US FP2 sleep-data retrieval has not been verified. No device was queried,
  and this experiment did not connect to Home Assistant or MQTT. The earlier
  non-FP2 query is not device-data evidence. China evidence remains limited
  to the request-signature checks recorded in the October 2 proof; no real
  China-account login is claimed.
